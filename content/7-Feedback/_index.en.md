> The following feedback reflects my experience in the **Workforce Bootcamp - First Cloud AI Journey** program and the development of the **EduFlow Platform** during the internship from **May 19, 2026** to **August 17, 2026**.

### General assessment

**1. Working and learning environment**

The program allowed me to combine self-directed learning with hands-on work on one continuous project. While developing EduFlow, I could evaluate possible solutions, verify their results, and take responsibility from source code through deployment. This approach matches software engineering and cloud computing, where knowledge should be demonstrated through a working system.

**2. Mentor and team-admin support**

The First Cloud AI Journey documentation, sample workshop, and report structure provided a clear reference for the internship. Requirements for worklogs, proposals, workshops, and evidence helped me identify which outcomes needed to be recorded rather than merely describing activities. The emphasis on independent investigation also strengthened my ability to read documentation, analyze logs, and troubleshoot before summarizing the result.

**3. Alignment with my academic major**

The internship was directly relevant to my **Software Engineering** major. EduFlow covered requirements analysis, architecture, Spring Boot/Thymeleaf development, MySQL, security, testing, source control, and cloud deployment. It therefore allowed me to apply university knowledge across a complete software-development lifecycle.

**4. Learning and skill-development opportunities**

The internship expanded my experience from application development to AWS operations. I practiced Docker, Terraform, Amazon ECS Fargate, Application Load Balancer, Amazon RDS, Secrets Manager, CloudWatch, and GitHub Actions. The 12-week worklog, bilingual report, and workshop documentation also improved my ability to present and organize technical outcomes.

**5. Learning culture and community spirit**

First Cloud AI Journey resources, workshop activities, and hackathons encouraged knowledge sharing and product development. Participating in KET, AABW, Qoder, and OpenAI hackathons exposed me to different problem-solving approaches and helped me turn ideas into products supported by repositories, documentation, and technical evidence.

**6. Internship support and program resources**

The most practical forms of support were the learning roadmap, reference materials, report template, and opportunity to deliver a complete cloud project. Being able to select and develop EduFlow helped me build a product aligned with my interests while meeting the shared internship requirements.

### Additional questions

- **What satisfied me most:** Taking end-to-end responsibility for EduFlow, from feature development to AWS deployment and a successful VNPay sandbox transaction test.
- **What could be improved for future interns:** Provide a unified checklist early for required deliverables, mandatory evidence, assessment criteria, and weekly review milestones so interns can plan from the beginning.
- **Would I recommend the program:** Yes, especially to Software Engineering students who want to combine software development with cloud, DevOps, and technical-documentation skills.

### Suggestions and expectations

- Add regular reviews focused on architecture, security, AWS cost, and evidence quality in addition to general progress checks.
- Provide minimal reference repositories for ECS Fargate, Terraform, CI/CD, observability, and secret management so interns share a consistent starting point.
- Continue First Cloud AI Journey and create more opportunities for interns to present products, receive technical feedback, and share experience with the next cohort.
